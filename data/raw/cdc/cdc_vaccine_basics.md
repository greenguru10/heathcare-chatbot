## How Vaccines Work
Vaccines help develop immunity by imitating an infection. When a vaccine is introduced, the immune system produces antibodies and T-lymphocytes that remember how to fight that pathogen in the future. The vaccine almost never causes illness because it uses weakened, inactive, or mRNA/protein subunit versions of the antigen.

## Why Vaccination is Important
- Vaccines protect individuals from dangerous diseases such as measles, mumps, rubella, polio, tetanus, hepatitis B, influenza, and HPV.
- Community immunity (herd immunity) protects vulnerable individuals who cannot be vaccinated, such as infants or people with severe immunodeficiencies.
- Vaccines have eliminated or dramatically reduced diseases that once killed or harmed millions of children and adults.

## Common Expected Side Effects
Most vaccine side effects are mild, temporary, and resolve within 1 to 3 days:
- Soreness, redness, or mild swelling at the injection site
- Low-grade fever
- Mild fatigue or muscle aches
- Headache or chills

## Safety and Red Flags
Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) following vaccination are extremely rare (approximately 2 to 5 cases per million doses). Signs of a severe allergic reaction include:
- Trouble breathing or wheezing
- Swelling of the throat, tongue, or face
- Hives or a widespread itchy rash
- Dizziness, paleness, or fainting

If signs of an allergic reaction occur after receiving a vaccine, call emergency services immediately or proceed to the nearest emergency room.
