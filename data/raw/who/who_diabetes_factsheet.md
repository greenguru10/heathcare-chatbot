# WHO Fact Sheet: Diabetes

**Source:** World Health Organization
**URL:** https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/diabetes
**Publication Date:** 2023-04-05
**Last Updated:** 2025-01-20
**Authority Tier:** A
**Category:** chronic_condition_education

## Overview and Definition
Diabetes is a chronic, metabolic disease characterized by elevated levels of blood glucose (or blood sugar), which leads over time to serious damage to the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys and nerves. The most common is type 2 diabetes, usually in adults, which occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or doesn't make enough insulin. Type 1 diabetes is a chronic condition in which the pancreas produces little or no insulin by itself.

## Common Symptoms
Symptoms of diabetes include:
- Feeling very thirsty
- Urinating more often than usual, especially at night
- Losing weight without trying
- Feeling very hungry
- Blurry vision
- Feeling very tired
- Having very dry skin
- Having sores that heal slowly
- Having more infections than usual

Symptoms of type 1 diabetes can develop quickly over weeks or months, while symptoms of type 2 diabetes often develop slowly over several years.

## Prevention
Type 1 diabetes cannot currently be prevented. Effective lifestyle approaches are available to prevent type 2 diabetes and its complications:
- Achieve and maintain a healthy body weight
- Be physically active – doing at least 30 minutes of regular, moderate-intensity activity on most days
- Eat a healthy diet, avoiding sugar and saturated fats
- Avoid tobacco use – smoking increases the risk of diabetes and cardiovascular disease

## When to Seek Medical Attention
If you notice persistent excessive thirst, frequent nighttime urination, unexplained weight loss, or persistent fatigue, consult a qualified healthcare provider for clinical blood glucose testing (such as fasting blood sugar or HbA1c). If blood sugar drops severely causing confusion, trembling, sweating, or loss of consciousness (hypoglycemia), or spikes causing deep rapid breathing, nausea, and fruity breath odor (possible ketoacidosis), seek immediate emergency medical care.
