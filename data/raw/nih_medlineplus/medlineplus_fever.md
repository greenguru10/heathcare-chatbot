## What is a Fever?
A fever is a temporary increase in your body temperature, often due to an illness. Having a fever is a sign that something out of the ordinary is going on in your body. For an adult or child, a fever usually indicates an oral or rectal temperature of 100.4°F (38.0°C) or higher.

## Causes of Fever
Fever is most commonly caused by:
- Viral infections (such as the common cold, influenza, COVID-19)
- Bacterial infections (such as strep throat, urinary tract infection, pneumonia)
- Inflammatory conditions
- Immunizations (mild post-vaccine temperature elevation)

## Home Comfort Measures
- Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths, oral rehydration solutions) to stay hydrated.
- Rest in a comfortable, well-ventilated room with lightweight clothing.
- Use a sponge bath with lukewarm (not cold) water if uncomfortable.
- Never give aspirin to children or teenagers due to the risk of Reye's syndrome.

## When to Seek Urgent Clinical Attention
In infants under 3 months: any rectal temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher requires immediate emergency medical evaluation, even with no other symptoms.
In children and adults, seek immediate emergency care if fever is accompanied by:
- Stiff neck and severe headache
- Difficulty breathing or rapid shallow breathing
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or unresponsiveness
- Seizure (convulsion)
- Purple or dark unexplained rash
- Signs of severe dehydration (no urination, sunken eyes, no tears)
