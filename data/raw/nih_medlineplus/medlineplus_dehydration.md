## What is Dehydration?
Dehydration happens when your body loses more fluids and electrolytes than you take in. Your body needs enough water to carry out its normal functions, such as regulating body temperature, lubricating joints, and flushing waste products.

## Causes of Dehydration
Common causes of dehydration include:
- Vigorous exercise in hot or humid weather without drinking enough fluids
- Severe, acute diarrhea and vomiting (e.g. gastroenteritis)
- High fever
- Increased urination due to unmanaged diabetes or diuretic medications
- Inadequate fluid intake, especially in infants, young children, and older adults

## Mild to Moderate Symptoms
- Thirst and dry, sticky mouth
- Urinating less often or producing dark amber-colored urine
- Fatigue or lightheadedness upon standing
- Dry skin
- Headache

## Severe Symptoms and Red Flags
Severe dehydration is a medical emergency. Red flags include:
- Not urinating for over 8 hours, or very dark/amber urine
- Extremely dry skin, sunken eyes, and lack of tears when crying
- Rapid heartbeat and rapid breathing
- Dizziness, confusion, lethargy, or fainting
- Inability to keep fluids or oral rehydration solutions down

## Prevention and Care
- Drink plenty of water throughout the day, especially before, during, and after physical activity.
- During hot weather or illness with fever, diarrhea, or vomiting, drink extra fluids, including oral rehydration solutions (ORS) that replenish sodium and potassium.
- If severe dehydration symptoms occur, seek emergency medical care immediately. Intravenous fluids in a clinic or hospital setting may be required.
